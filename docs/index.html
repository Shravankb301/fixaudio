Why is the volume slider greyed out on my Mac?

Because the audio is travelling over your display cable. When sound goes out over HDMI or DisplayPort to a monitor, macOS treats the volume as the monitor’s business rather than its own, and disables its own control entirely — the Control Center slider greys out and the keyboard volume keys do nothing at all.

The monitor is still listening, though. Most of them accept DDC/CI commands over that same cable. fixaudio writes VCP code 0x62 — audio speaker volume — straight to the display, and intercepts your media keys to drive it.

Mac mini and Mac Studio HDMI: the case nothing else covers

If you searched for this because MonitorControl doesn’t work on your Mac mini, here’s why. Macs with a built-in HDMI port don’t emit HDMI natively — they emit DisplayPort and convert it with an MCDP2900 chip. That chip can do the I2C communication DDC needs, but Apple cuts it off at firmware level once the display’s EDID has been read.

So MonitorControl detects and disables itself on the built-in HDMI port of the 2018 Intel Mac mini, all M1 Macs, and the entry-level M2 Mac mini. Its official advice is to use USB-C instead.

On the M4 Mac mini, DDC writes over the built-in HDMI port work. That’s what fixaudio was built on and verified against — an M4 Mac mini driving a Samsung CF791 over plain HDMI, with working volume keys.

Verified on exactly one machine so far — mine. If you have an Apple Silicon Mac mini or Studio, running ddcvol doctor and reporting the result genuinely helps establish where this works and where it doesn’t.

What you get

  • The volume keys work. F11, F12 and mute, with an on-screen level readout.
  • They still work everywhere else. When output isn’t a monitor, keys pass straight through and macOS behaves exactly as it always has.
  • Fast output switching. ⌘⌥⌃A cycles devices, and a 🔈 menubar item picks one directly.
  • A scriptable CLI. ddcvol set 40 in a shell script or Shortcut — something the GUI apps don’t give you.

It works on monitors that lie about their own state

Plenty of monitors accept DDC writes but return garbage on reads. The Samsung CF791 this was built for reports the same value for every setting, and claims its maximum volume is -128.

Tools that read the current volume before changing it get stuck on hardware like this. fixaudio never reads. It caches the level locally, per display, and only ever writes.

It then re-asserts that level after sleep, input changes, and device switches — because a monitor won’t remember it for you, and its own buttons will move it behind your back.

The CLI

Mostly you just press the volume keys. Beyond that:

ddcvol up / downRaise or lower the volume
ddcvol set <0-100>Set an absolute level
ddcvol muteToggle mute
ddcvol syncRe-push the cached level to the monitor
ddcvol statusShow the current route and level
ddcvol doctorDiagnose DDC support on your machine
audioout cycleSwitch to the next output device
audioout set <name>Switch to one by name

Questions

Why don’t my volume keys work with my HDMI monitor?

macOS disables its own volume control whenever audio is routed over a display cable, because it assumes the display owns the volume. Nothing is broken — it’s deliberate. fixaudio works around it by talking to the monitor directly over DDC/CI.

Will this work on my monitor?

Run ddcvol doctor after installing — it tells you whether your display answers DDC at all, which port it’s on, and whether its read channel is reliable. DDC volume support is firmware-dependent and genuinely hit-or-miss between models, so the diagnostic is the honest answer.

How is this different from MonitorControl?

MonitorControl is the well-known app here and does far more, including brightness. Try it first. fixaudio covers the narrower cases it doesn’t: monitors whose DDC reads return garbage, Mac mini built-in HDMI ports, and people who want a scriptable CLI instead of an app.

What about BetterDisplay?

BetterDisplay also handles HDMI DDC on these Macs and is free — if you want a full-featured display app, use it. fixaudio is two shell scripts and a Hammerspoon module, for people who want the volume keys fixed and nothing else running.

Does it slow down my volume keys?

No. The decision about where to send a keypress is cached per output device rather than computed per press, and rapid repeats are coalesced into a single DDC write, so holding the key doesn’t queue up round-trips.

Does it break my built-in speakers?

No. When the output device isn’t a DDC display, keypresses pass through untouched and macOS handles them exactly as before.

Is it safe to pipe the installer to bash?

Read it first — it’s short and on GitHub. It installs three Homebrew packages, copies two scripts to ~/.local/bin, and appends one line to your Hammerspoon config (backing it up first). uninstall.sh reverses it.

Before you install

fixaudio installs m1ddc, switchaudio-osx and Hammerspoon via Homebrew. Hammerspoon needs Accessibility permission to see your volume keys.

DDC doesn’t work over every port on Apple Silicon. If ddcvol doctor finds no displays, try a different cable or port — USB-C and DisplayPort are the most reliable.
